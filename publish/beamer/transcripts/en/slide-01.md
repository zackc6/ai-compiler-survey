# Slide 1: plain

Welcome the room and set the frame before any evidence.

**Title and subtitle.**
Read the headline: *Next-Gen AI Compiler.* Subtitle: *Agents On The Control Plane.* That split is the whole briefing — not “LLM replaces the compiler,” but agents reshape *how* optimization is searched and shipped.

**The spine in one breath.**
Agents own **search** (explore pass order, kernel variants, heuristics), **orchestration** (multi-step workflows, tool routing, freeze and replay), and **synthesis** (emit hints, control files, shippable C++ heuristics). Compilers own **lowering** (graph → IR → kernels → machine code), **legality** (what transforms are allowed), **measure** (profiles, microbenchmarks, build-CI), and **fallback** (classical path when an agent proposal fails admit). Say it cleanly: hybrid **control plane** over a classical **data plane** — the survey’s settled lean.

**The ask.**
Plant the question you want them answering by the end: is the **hybrid prediction** for roughly **2027–31** the right bet for *their* roadmap? Not “will agents exist?” — they already do in labs — but whether to budget for typed admit, oracles, freeze artifacts, and CI-gated specialize now versus waiting for a single mega-model to eat LLVM.

**Timing and contract.**
Footer cue: expert briefing, about **30–35 minutes** plus Q&A. **Prediction-first** — diagrams and falsifiable checkpoints carry the argument; org tags on later slides are evidence, not decoration. You are not selling a product; you are pressure-testing a thesis they can take back to planning.
